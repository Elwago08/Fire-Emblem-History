A Brief History of the Fire Emblem Series

This page will talk about how the gaming series started, how it almost died, and where it is today, split into 3 main sections:

  1. The Beginning
  2. Expansion and Crisis
  3. Revival

The Beginning

The series started as a passion project by Shouzou Kaga, who was a developer at Nintendo at the time. The first game, Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light, ended up selling better than expected, which led to him becoming director of the new series. Kaga would then go on to keep working on the series for roughly the next decade.

Shouzou Kaga ended up on working the first 5 games of the series before leaving Nintendo, those games being:

  • Fire Emblem: Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light
  • Fire Emblem: Gaiden
  • Fire Emblem: Mystery of the Emblem
  • Fire Emblem: Genealogy of the Holy War
  • Fire Emblem: Thracia 776

Expansion and Crisis

With Kaga gone, Nintendo had to figure out how to keep making these games. There was an attempt to make one on the Nintendo 64, but it never ended up being finished. Eventually, they were able to restablish the series and continue it's growing popularity. The series how now become so popular that Nintendo finally decided to release the series outside of Japan. The first game to be released internationally was Fire Emblem: The Blazing Blade, or more often called "Fire Emblem." The series didn't struggle to capture international audiences at first, but the love of the series started to fade. Sales were going down, and Nintendo had told Intelligent Systems, the main development studio behind the series, that the next game had to sell over 250,000 units, or the series would be cut.
